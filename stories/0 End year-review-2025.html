2025 Year in Review: A Landmark Year for CRCRN

Annual Review | December 2025

A Year of Beginning and Growth

2025 has been an eventful start for the Coral Reef Conservation and Restoration Network. From expanding our collaborative footprint and research, our team and partners have given us valuable opportunities listen, learn, and reflect on how we can collectively care for our ecosystems. We begin to make significant strides in advancing reef science and conservation practice across the region.

A Snapshot of 2025

This year brought a welcomed growth. We expanded engagement to island of Siquijor, Negros Island Region (Negros Oriental). Our monitoring study led by Dr. Maypa was published in MetroPost Dumaguete and Silliman University - Sillimanian Weekly, establishing critical baseline data and awareness for tracking ecosystem health. Additionally, our capacity building efforts have expanded or collaborative efforts with RRReefs, SUIEMS, etc for engagements, monitoring and data collection.

Workshops and Research Engagement

Earlier this year, our project lead, Dr. Aileen Maypa, participated in international marine conservation workshops as part of the 2025 Pew Marine Fellows program. From Hong Kong to Galway, Ireland, these gatherings provided space for sharing experiences, discussing community engagement strategies, and learning from other marine scientists around the world. Insights from these events help inform CRCRN's research and community-based restoration work. Read more:1↗,2↗

Coral Reef Conservation and Restoration News

Apo Island Jetty Port Updates: The coral reefs of Apo Island are a prime example of community-led conservation. In August, a community debriefing brought together local stakeholders, researchers, and conservationists to discuss the implications of the jetty port to the community and the environment, and DENR's decision to halt this project last July 2025.
